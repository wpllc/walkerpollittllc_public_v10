When the Whole Damn Town Is Booked

Finding affordable accommodations when a major construction project overwhelms a small town.

Walker Pollitt LLC · November 27, 2026

A new industrial facility, data center, power plant or infrastructure project can bring hundreds of temporary workers into a community that was never designed to accommodate them.

Suddenly, finding somewhere to sleep becomes a project of its own.

For traveling engineers, technicians and contractors, the solution is not always finding the cheapest hotel. It is finding the most economical arrangement for the entire assignment.

Stop searching by city name alone

Start with the actual jobsite location and investigate surrounding communities.

A hotel in a neighboring town might be substantially less expensive. But distance alone does not tell the whole story.

Consider road conditions, construction delays, seasonal weather, shift schedules and whether your commute will be reasonable after a long workday.

A 40-mile interstate commute and a 40-mile rural mountain commute can be completely different experiences.

Call the property directly

Online booking platforms are useful for discovering properties, but they do not always display every extended-stay arrangement a property may offer.

Ask the property manager about weekly or monthly rates, corporate agreements, kitchens, laundry facilities, parking, internet access and early departure policies.

Also verify whether the room is available for your entire assignment rather than merely the first few nights.

Get the final terms in writing before committing.

Calculate what the room actually costs

Consider two hypothetical lodging options for a 28-night assignment involving 20 workdays.

ExpenseHotel AHotel B
Nightly rate$145$95
Room cost$4,060$2,660
Daily round-trip commute10 miles60 miles
Total commuting distance200 miles1,200 miles
Estimated vehicle expense at $0.50/mile$100$600
Combined expense$4,160$3,260

Hotel B saves $900 before taxes, additional fees and the value of commuting time.

If that longer commute adds one hour per workday, however, you are also sacrificing 20 hours of personal time.

At that point, the decision becomes more complicated than comparing room rates.

The mileage expense above is an illustrative planning assumption, not an official reimbursement or tax-deduction rate.

Build a repeatable lodging database

Rather than starting over every time you receive a new assignment, maintain a simple spreadsheet.

Record the property, town, quoted rate, taxes, parking, commuting distance, kitchen availability, internet reliability, cancellation conditions and date verified.

If you maintain separate CSV files for different assignments, Excel's Power Query can combine similarly structured files into a consolidated dataset.

Over time, this creates a useful personal reference for negotiating lodging, estimating assignment expenses and identifying properties worth returning to.

Understand your reimbursement arrangement

A fixed daily allowance, actual-expense reimbursement and employer-paid lodging are different arrangements.

Do not assume that the federal government lodging allowance automatically applies to private-sector employment.

Even for federal travel, hotels are not obligated to offer government rates. Your actual reimbursement depends on the applicable travel rules and authorization.

Consult your employer's written travel policy before making a substantial nonrefundable reservation.

Consider alternatives without committing prematurely

Furnished apartments, extended-stay hotels, independently operated motels and RV parks may all deserve consideration.

For longer assignments, having a kitchen and laundry facilities can significantly change your overall expenses.

Verify availability, safety, utility arrangements and cancellation terms directly. An inexpensive listing is not necessarily a suitable place to live for three months.

The objective is not simply to survive on the road. It is to make traveling for work financially worthwhile without destroying your quality of life in the process.
